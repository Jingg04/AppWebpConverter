# Why Client-Side In-Browser Conversion is 100% Private (Zero Server Uploads)

<VisualAsset id="va-93-1" type="hero" src="/public/blog-assets/93-why-client-side-browser-conversion-is-100-percent-private/hero.svg" alt="3D architectural render of private client-side WebAssembly image processing sandbox with zero network uploads" />

> **Summary:** Most free online file converters secretly upload and store your sensitive photos on remote servers. Discover why client-side WebAssembly processing is 100% private.




## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do traditional online converters handle my uploaded files?

Legacy converters require your browser to upload the entire image file to their remote cloud servers. Those servers execute ImageMagick or FFmpeg, save the output to temporary cloud storage buckets, and generate a download link. Your private files sit on foreign hard drives.

### How does FluxWebP convert images without uploading them?

FluxWebP compiles native C++ and Rust image codecs (like libwebp and mozjpeg) into WebAssembly (WASM). When you drop an image, the WebAssembly engine runs locally inside your browser's V8 JavaScript engine. Data never leaves your device's local memory.

### How can I independently prove that FluxWebP does not upload my files?

Open Chrome DevTools (F12) → go to the <strong>Network</strong> tab. Convert a 10MB photo. You will see exactly ZERO outbound HTTP POST or PUT requests carrying image data. The processing works even if you disconnect your Wi-Fi entirely.
