## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between destructive and non-destructive editing?

Destructive editing overwrites raw pixel data directly in memory—once saved, original image information is lost forever. Non-destructive editing stores modifications as a series of parametric instructions or independent layers applied over the untouched original asset.

### How does the undo history stack work in FluxWebP?

FluxWebP maintains an immutable command history stack. Each action (crop, filter, text add) pushes a lightweight state command. Pressing Ctrl+Z rewinds the stack and re-renders the pipeline from the original buffer without quality degradation.

### Does non-destructive editing consume more RAM?

Storing multiple full 4K pixel buffers can consume memory. FluxWebP solves this by storing mathematical operation parameters rather than cloned bitmaps, keeping memory footprint under 50MB.
