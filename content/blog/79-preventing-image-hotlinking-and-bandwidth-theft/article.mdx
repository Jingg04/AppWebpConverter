# Preventing Image Hotlinking and Bandwidth Theft with Server Headers

<VisualAsset id="va-79-1" type="hero" src="/public/blog-assets/79-preventing-image-hotlinking-and-bandwidth-theft/hero.svg" alt="3D visual of cybersecurity firewall blocking image hotlinking and unauthorized bandwidth theft" />

> **Summary:** Don't pay for other people's traffic. Learn how third parties hotlink your photos, and how to block bandwidth theft with HTTP Referer validation without hurting SEO.




## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is image hotlinking?

Hotlinking (bandwidth theft) occurs when an external website embeds an image hosted on your server directly into their web pages using your URL (<code>&lt;img src='https://yourdomain.com/photo.webp'&gt;</code>). Every time their page loads, your server pays for the bandwidth.

### Will anti-hotlink protection block Google Images from crawling?

Not if configured correctly. Safe anti-hotlinking rules explicitly whitelist search engine crawler bots (Googlebot, Bingbot) and search referrers (google.com, bing.com), ensuring your SEO rankings are completely preserved.

### What should you return when a hotlinked request is blocked?

Return an HTTP 403 Forbidden status, or serve an informative branding image (e.g. 'Image hosted by YourBrand - Visit us at yourdomain.com') to convert unauthorized traffic into brand awareness.
