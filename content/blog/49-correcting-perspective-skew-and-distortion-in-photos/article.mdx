> **Summary:** Stop building photos from looking like they're falling backward. Master perspective transformation, keystoning correction, and 4-point mesh warping.




## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is keystoning in architectural photography?

Keystoning is the geometric perspective distortion that occurs when a camera is tilted upward to fit a tall building. Parallel vertical walls appear to converge toward a vanishing point, making the building look like it is falling backward.

### How does 4-point perspective warping work?

A projective transformation matrix (homography) maps four arbitrary corner points from the distorted quadrangle onto a true rectangular grid, straightening converging vertical lines mathematically.

### Does perspective correction cause image cropping?

Yes. Straightening keystoning flares the top corners of the image outward. The resulting canvas must be cropped slightly to eliminate transparent corner triangular gaps.
