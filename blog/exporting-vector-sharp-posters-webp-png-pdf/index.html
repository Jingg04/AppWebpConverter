Spending hours crafting a stunning marketing poster only to have your exported file turn into a pixelated, fuzzy mess is heartbreaking. Screen previews can be deceiving: a canvas that looks crisp on your laptop can blur when shared on social media or sent to a commercial printer. Mastering canvas export multipliers and format selection is the final crucial step of poster production.

The Format Selection Decision Tree

  • Print-Ready PDF (Vector PDF/X): Required for physical print shops. Preserves typographic letterforms and logo shapes as infinite mathematical Bezier curves.
  • Modern WebP (Quality 85-92%): The ultimate choice for websites, landing pages, and email blasts. Delivers razor-sharp 4K resolution under 2MB.
  • Lossless 24-bit PNG: Recommended when you need an uncompressed raster archival master or transparent alpha background for video editing overlays.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do posters designed on a 1080p screen look blurry when exported?

If an editor exports only at screen pixel resolution (1x DPR), you get an asset that lacks density for large monitors or physical print. FluxWebP uses a 2x or 4x render multiplier, re-rendering vector text and shapes at 4K or 8K resolution upon export.

When should I export as PDF instead of WebP?

Export as PDF/X whenever you are sending the file to a physical commercial print shop. PDF preserves text and shapes as mathematical vectors, allowing infinite magnification with zero pixelation.

Why is WebP better than PNG for sharing posters on the web?

A 4K PNG poster averages 12MB to 20MB, making it slow to download and share. High-quality WebP produces identical visual sharpness at just 1.5MB to 2.5MB—saving 85% bandwidth.
