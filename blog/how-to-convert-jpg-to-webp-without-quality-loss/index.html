How to Convert JPG to WebP Without Unnecessary Quality Loss

Converting from one lossy format (JPEG) to another (WebP) requires careful calibration. Because lossy codecs discard imperceptible high-frequency frequencies, setting the quality threshold too low can cause compounded generational compression artifacts. Follow this guide to achieve maximum byte savings with zero perceptual loss.

The Sweet Spot: The 82% Quality Rule

Extensive perceptual testing proves that a WebP quality setting of 82% delivers the ideal balance for digital photography. At 82%, WebP retains fine skin texture, hair strands, and subtle fabric weaves while discarding redundant spatial noise, cutting overall file weight by roughly a third.
