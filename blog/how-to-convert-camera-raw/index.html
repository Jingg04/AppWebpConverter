How to Convert Camera RAW (ARW, CR2, NEF, DNG) in Browser

Updated: November 2026 • Reading time: 5 min • By FluxWebP Imaging Architecture

Digital photographers shooting on Sony Alpha, Canon EOS/R, Nikon Z, or Leica systems capture photos in uncompressed 14-bit or 16-bit Camera RAW formats. These files preserve full sensor dynamic range, but each photo can weigh between 30MB and 120MB.

Until recently, viewing or converting RAW files required expensive desktop software like Adobe Lightroom or Capture One. Today, modern Web APIs and Web Workers allow browsers to parse and convert RAW files locally in milliseconds.

1. The Architecture of Camera RAW Files

Most Camera RAW formats are structurally based on the TIFF (Tagged Image File Format) standard or ISOBMFF (ISO Base Media File Format):

  • Sony (.ARW): TIFF container holding raw bayer mosaic data and an embedded full-resolution ISP JPEG.
  • Canon (.CR2 / .CR3): CR2 uses TIFF; CR3 uses modern QuickTime/MP4 box structures with high-efficiency previews.
  • Nikon (.NEF): Extended TIFF specification containing camera picture controls and EXIF color matrices.
  • Adobe (.DNG): Open standard digital negative format created by Adobe Systems.

2. How FluxWebP Pro Decodes RAW on Your Local Machine

When you drop an ARW or CR2 file into FluxWebP Pro:

  1. The browser creates an in-memory ArrayBuffer slice without uploading bytes over the internet.
  2. A specialized parser scans binary tags for JPEG SOI markers (0xFF 0xD8) and sub-IFD offset tags.
  3. The full-resolution hardware-processed ISP preview is extracted instantly.
  4. HTML5 Canvas re-encodes the buffer into lightweight WebP, AVIF, or JPEG at your desired quality.
